ASURYANI:
Troops
With the already meagre
population of the Craftworlds
continuing to decline and the
Aspect Shrines in their relative
infancy, it became necessary
for the Aeldari to raise militia
levies to serve as the mainstay
of their armies. It was a
testament to the prowess of
the Aeldari that, despite these
militias being ostensibly
composed of civilians,
Guardians proved more than
capable of standing against
the Craftworlds myriad
enemies time and again.
Guardian Defenders were the
most common configuration
of Guardian squad
encountered, serving a
battlefield role not dissimilar
to the basic line infantry of
humanity’s armies. Defenders
acted in support of the more
specialised, offensivelyfocused Aspect Warriors and
Storm Guardians, claiming
ground and laying down
withering hailstorms of fire.
Guardian Defenders
nonetheless appeared wary of
allowing the enemy to close
into melee with them, always
ready to manoeuvre to
another position and deny
their enemy a chance to close
into melee.
In addition to their standard
armament of Shuriken
Catapults and Plasma
Grenades, Guardian
Defenders regularly crewed
heavy weapon platforms,
acting as mobile fire-bases
that suppressed or outright
destroyed many a foe.

Comparatively rarer than
Defender squads, Storm
Defenders were called upon
when the grim calculus of war
required ever-conservative
Aeldari commanders to
nonetheless commit dedicated
shock troops to the fiercest
sectors of the battle.
Appropriately, many Storm
Guardians were drawn from
those civilian Aeldari with
notable experience of war, but
who were not otherwise
serving as officers.
Storm Guardians forwent
Shuriken Catapults in favour
of pistols and close-combat
weaponry, better suited to the
bloody carnage of melee,
though some were afforded
more specialised armaments
such as flamers and Fusion
Blasters, apparently
uncoupled from the rigidity of
their Defender counterparts,
perhaps befitting their history
as warriors.

Those who fought the Eldar
during the Great Crusade
would often speak of their
speed and powerful weapons.
This was embodied at most in
their Windriders, highly
mobile jetbike formations
mounting powerful shuriken
or laser weaponry. Some
Craftworlds fielded these as
hereditary family units, or
‘Windrider Clans’, and were
capable of bringing hundreds
or even thousands of such
vehicles on to bear on the field
at a time.
Only the small populations of
post-fall craftworlds kept
these formations from
overwhelming Imperial
outposts and agri-worlds
unchallenged. Imperial
commanders frequently had
to call in aerial assets or
Legiones Astartes forces to
hunt down Eldar Windriders,
so manoeuvrable were they.

The Dire Avengers were the
first among the nascent
Aspect Shrines, as well as the
most common, for Asurmen
founded more Shrines on
more Craftworlds than any
other Phoenix Lord. They
were regarded as the
protectors and the avengers of
their Craftworlds; the
representation of Khaine as a
noble warrior.
They were the least specialised
of the Aspect Warriors,
affording them greater
tactical flexibility in their role
as elite ranged infantry, often
acting in support of Guardian
squads at the heart of a
conflict – rapidly shifting
between offensive and defence
tactics as necessary, for they
possessed a seemingly
preternatural ability to read
the ebb and flow of battle.
While the Shuriken Catapult
was a firearm synonymous
with the Asuryani, the Dire
Avengers were deemed the
masters of its use – its
wielding an art-form.
Although numerous variants
existed, the more
sophisticated Avenger-pattern
was unique to these Aspect
Warriors. Although the Dire
Avengers eschewed the
carnage of melee, they were
no less deadly for it, as all
carried ceremonial knives as a
weapon of last resort.

The foundation of the
Asuryani is the Path. The path
is not just a synonym for
profession; far more than
simply that, it embodies a
series of restrictions and
guides that work with the
Eldar mind to contain its
power and psychic abilities,
channelling them into a
productive and effective state.
Even in the early days of the
Craftworlds, perhaps
especially so, there were many
who found this restrictive
framework chaffing. With the
empire fallen, the navy
scattered, and the Exodites
quietly isolating themselves,
the Craftworlds became a
major way-point for these
roving wanderers in their
travels throughout the galaxy.
To protect themselves in their
journeys, these Rangers
frequently carried Long-Rifles,
to stop threats before they
were detected, and purchased
as much with vital
information as they did with
relics of a bygone era brought
to the last cradles of their
people. In time most of these
wayfarers would find a home
and settle into a path of their
choosing, but in the
meantime, their welcomes
had to be kept short so as to
not draw the eye of the ever
hungry malignity of She Who
Thirsts.

The Wave Serpent was one of
the more common forms of
transport available to the
Asuryani. Being both fast and
capable of traversing the
majority of gates in the
webway network, the Wave
Serpent traded much of the
firepower seen in similar
vehicles for approximately
twice the transport bay. While
this in and of itself was
undoubtedly of great use to
the early Craftworlds, the
lower energy requirements
also allowed it to mount a
potent energy shield known as
the Serpent Shield, and it was
from this vehicle drew its
name.
The Serpent was a guardian
figure in Eldar mythology, as
well as a deadly creature. The
Serpent Shield embodies this
duality, both reducing the
impact of incoming fire and
storing the kinetic energy of
attacks to unleash in a
terrifying wave known as a
Serpent Strike. While this
discharge frequently disabled
the system for a short time,
the effect was noted to pin
entrenched forces to their
fortifications or knock down
those in the open field. This
provided openings for the
Eldar warriors inside to
disembark and strike
unopposed.
