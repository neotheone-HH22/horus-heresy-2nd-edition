Wraithknights were towering
constructs, eclipsing even the
size and might of a
Wraithlord with their sheer
bulk. Many of these knights
were taken from the ruins of
Anarathe warships in the
aftermath of the Fall,
repurposed as guardians of
their respective Craftworlds.
Despite their size, they could
run across the battlefield,
their long-legged gait making
them faster than even a
charging Questoris Knight -
whilst their array of ancient
weaponry allowed them to
unleash the fury of the Aeldari
at a moment’s notice.
Wraithknights provided a
powerful middle ground
position between the smaller
Wraithlords and
Wraithguards, and the much
larger Titan sized constructs
that stood sentinel over the
largest and most important
shrines of the Aeldari.
The Wraithknights differed
from their smaller kin with
the fact that along with the
soul of a slain Eldar, each
Wraithknight was also piloted
by an living Eldar - and at
that, one which was their
twin. Such a rare occurrence
lead to a strong psychic link
between the two pilots, to the
point that the still-living twin
was occasionally entombed
within the cockpit
permanently, bonding their
souls and mind beyond death,
and allowing the
wraithknight to move with
the speed and grace of a living
Eldar.

Specialised for hunting the
tunnels of the webway, the
Skathach Wraithknights were
a rare and terrible breed of
Wraithknight. Piloted by a
living Eldar separated
permanently from their twin,
they patrolled the endless
tunnels of the Webway using
esoteric weaponry similar in
design to those of the Fire
Dragon Aspect Warriors.
These pilots spent most of
their time communing with
the soul of their wraithknight
as they defended the webway
from any that would trespass,
bonding with it in a strange
symbiosis. Rarely emerging
into realspace, the rare
occasions in which they were
seen to appear were typically
in defence of a webway portal
under threat, or otherwise to
aid an Eldar force upon the
brink of defeat. Multiple
hunting bands and small
armies were been rescued by
the sudden appearance of a
Skathach Wraithknight after
they had met with forces that
grossly outnumbered and
overpowered their own.
Along with their normal
armament, the Skathach
mounted Webway Shunt
Generators, immensely
powerful engines which
allowed them to slip in and
out of the webway seemingly
with a ghost-like grace;
keeping a solid location lock
on them proving near
impossible in a battle
condition. This would often
end only one way for their
foes - for when they
reappeared in realspace,
cannons shining bright, they
would lay a foe low before
simply melting back into the
webway to continue their
patrols.

The Eldar Vampire chassis
was already respected as a
harbinger of oncoming
assault elements in its Raider
form, but rarer and far more
feared was the deadly
Vampire Hunter variant.
Exchanging its transport
capacity to support the
systems required to mount
titan-grade pulsars, the
Vampire Hunter was capable
of destroying the largest and
most powerful machines of
the Imperium. Indeed, its
destruction was prioritised
over all others by the agents of
the Mechanicum for its ability
to strike against their Titans,
whose anti-air weaponry was
far to slow to track the nimble
craft during their attack runs.
Thankfully for the lesser races
of the galaxy, after the fall,
the number of Vampire
Hunters still in use was
exceptionally small. Building
new Pulsars was a timeconsuming process that
required specialist engineers
and space that was at a
premium on all but the largest
of the nascent Craftworlds. As
such, many Asuryani
commanders only committed
their Vampire Hunters to the
skies as a last resort, or when
air superiority was so
thoroughly established as to
be a foregone conclusion.

Another example of the Eldar
mastery of anti-gravitic
technology, the Vampire
Raider was a void capable
strike craft capable of
carrying multiple units of
Eldar warriors hundreds of
thousands of kilometres
through the void, making
planetfall, and then
supporting them on the
battlefield. In this role, the
Vampire Raider saw use by
the Craftworlds, the remnants
of the Anrathe, and the evergrowing threat of Aeldari
Pirates during the Great
Crusade. This was
particularly troublesome for
local authorities in Imperial
systems, as their long range
and small profile for a void
ship meant that locating their
bases, or indeed webway
portals, was nearly impossible.
Often supported by smaller
fighter craft, the Vampire
Raider was still exceptionally
nimble for a vessel its size;
making it a difficult target for
local air defences and a
favoured transport for Eldar
commanders looking to make
landfall in force on a world
without safely charted
webway portals.

commonly the priority target

one. Yet, despite this, the foes
of the Eldar had to be wary,
for where one Revenant was
seen, a second was often not
far behind...
