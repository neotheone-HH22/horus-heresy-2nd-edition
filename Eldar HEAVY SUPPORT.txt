As light permeates everywhere
in the cosmos, so too did most
species eventually weaponize
it using laser technology. The
Eldar of the Craftworlds did
so with their expected
potency, mounting massive
focusing crystals on the turret
of their Falcon chassis. This
setup was known as the Fire
Prism, and mounted
additional batteries and
generation systems to power
the hungry demands of this
truly awe-inspiring laser
cannon.
Indeed, these focusing crystals
were capable of directing far
more energy than the Fire
Prism was capable of creating
itself. The earliest pilots were
quick to take advantage of
this, leveraging this and the
advanced targeting systems
of the chassis to fire directly
into the crystals of other fire
prisms, in effect combining
their firepower into a larger
and more terrible blast
capable of damaging even the
God-Machines of the
Mechanicum. It was from this
exceptionally dangerous and
deadly technique that the Fire
Prism drew its name, their
crystals blazing on every facet.

The falcon chassis was a
workhorse frontier vehicle in
many ways similar to the
Imperial Rhino Transport.
Adaptable for many worlds,
and capable of moving both
Eldar and materials swiftly
and securely to new places, it
was modified in countless
ways. One such modification
seen more frequently near the
time of the 30th millennium
was the Firestorm. Trading
out the flexible falcon turret
for a powerful and long
ranged ‘Firestorm’ Scatter
Laser Array, these vehicles
were capable of filling the sky
with potent anti-aircraft fire
to stop both alien raiders and
aerial wildlife from
approaching Eldar convoys
on the ground.
While the Craftworlds would
eventually phase these tanks
out, regarding them as a
second-line unit due to their
phenomenal aircraft, in the
days shortly after the fall the
chaos of the webway and the
shattering of supply lines
meant that these tanks
provided an important stopgap in their new military.
Unlike the hyper-specialised
Aspect Shrines, any on the
path of the Guardian could
operate these potent weapons,
providing an invaluable shield
to their fellows.

The Wraithlords were the
grander kin of the smaller
wraithguard and
wraithblades, standing tall far
above their still-living
brethren. Only the most
capable of the warrior dead
could be summoned from the
newly-created Infinity
Circuits to be attached to
these towering constructs.
Such an act was only to be
done in times of direst need,
as disturbing the rest of the
souls of the slain to rouse
them to war once more was
seen as nothing short of abject
defilement of their well earned
slumber.
Many of these ancient
constructs owed their origin
to the armouries of the
Anrathe, in the same manner
as their smaller wraith-kin,
the hallowed chassis they
inhabited having fought since
before the Fall of their ancient
race. Some Craftworlds make
greater use of these ancient
warriors than others, with
some using them in numbers
unheard of on others, and
reviled on many.
Armed with a variety of heavy
weaponry, a individual
Wraithlord can be equipped
for most any task, and
whichever way they were
armed, it could be assured
than they would perform with
utmost surety; whether
splitting foes from afar with
shuriken fire or beams from
their bright lances, or cutting
them apart in close combat
with immense swords or
bludgeoning them into the
ground with their gauntlets.

A Wraithseer was a sight very
rarely seen upon the
battlefields of the Great
Crusade or Heresy, as the
precious souls entombed
within them were guarded
jealously by the kin that
carried their legacy. In times
of utmost need, an especially
powerful Warlock could be
called forth from the Infinity
Circuit, and asked to stride to
war once more.
Such a request was not made
lightly, for if the wraithbone
construct that housed such a
venerable soul fell in battle,
then the soul within risked
being forever lost to the
predations of She Who
Thirsts. Despite this obvious
and immense risk, however,
such a construct was a
terrifying force that could
turn the tide of even the most
desperate battle, and so this
risk was deemed necessary on
occasion.
The armaments of the
Wraithseers were similar to
the more common
Wraithlords, making them a
considerable threat at range;
though the unique weapons
and wargear the Wraithseers
carried, in conjunction with
their considerable psychic
might, made them a deadly
adversary. Indeed, this psychic
might helped them overcome
the 'Wraithsight' that blighted
other wraiths, lending them a
greater speed and acuity - and
making it a deadly mistake to
underestimate the speed and
agility of a Wraithseer - and
not one many make twice.
